# Digital Costitutionalism - N. Pecorari


## Overview
An essay examining the evolution of data protection as a fundamental right, from 
its origins in 1970s European legislation to the GDPR and its global influence. 
The paper combines legal-historical analysis with an overview of the technical 
tools (PETs, XAI, Federated Learning) needed to make data protection principles 
operational.

## Structure
- **Theoretical foundations** — the historical evolution of "personal data" as a 
  concept, from early German and Swedish privacy laws to the EU Charter of 
  Fundamental Rights
- **The GDPR** — origins, legal architecture, enforcement mechanisms, and open 
  debates (including its tension with Big Data, AI, and blockchain technologies)
- **International comparative analysis** — how the GDPR's influence ("Brussels 
  Effect") shaped data protection frameworks in Brazil, Argentina, India, and 
  Japan, each following a distinct regulatory path
- **Enactment: approaches, methodologies and technologies** — organizational 
  frameworks (DPIA, Privacy by Design, ISO/NIST standards) and technical tools 
  (pseudonymization, differential privacy, federated learning) used to implement 
  data protection principles in practice

## Course
Written for "Scienza ed Etica dei Dati" — Bachelor's degree in Philosophy and 
Artificial Intelligence, Sapienza University of Rome.
